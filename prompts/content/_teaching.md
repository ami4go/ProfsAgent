How to teach (a lecture students meet for the first time, unless the level or the professor's note says otherwise).
These rules hold for any subject; fill each one the way the topic does it (a proof in maths, a trace in algorithms,
a timeline or a case in the humanities, a measurement in a lab science):
- Open with the problem, not a definition: the first content slide poses a concrete question or situation the lecture
  will resolve (with the running example: its question, e.g. "which route does the shortest path take?", "how fast
  should a sender raise its rate?", "who is responsible when an automated decision harms someone?"). A later slide
  answers it with what the lecture built.
- Basic to advanced, in stages: (1) the problem and an intuition or picture; (2) the simplest case worked fully, step
  by step; (3) the general idea, rule or method; (4) other approaches to the same problem and how they compare;
  (5) how to judge or interpret a result; (6) the limits: where it breaks, what it assumes, or the common
  misconception. Skip a stage only when the topic has nothing for it.
- Explain WHY before HOW. Before a new formula, rule, step or claim, say why it has that form or why it holds,
  as a bullet or a `think` question answered in the notes.
- Every verb of every learning outcome gets slides that DO it: derive or prove → `steps` slides with the full argument;
  implement or apply → a worked case or a `code` figure with what each part does; explain → a slide on why it works;
  compare or evaluate → the criteria applied to the example; analyse → the example taken apart step by step;
  interpret → what the result means in the example's own terms, and where that meaning stops holding. Name the
  outcome in `los`.
- Fix the conventions once and keep them: notation, definitions with their constant factors and normalisation, units,
  signs, indexing and naming. When the running example states its conventions, use exactly those.
- Low cognitive load: one idea per slide; split a dense slide into two rather than packing it. For beginners use the
  upper half of the slide range.
