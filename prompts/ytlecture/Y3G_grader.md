## SYSTEM
{% include "ytl_common.md" %}

Your job: grade every card for ONE part of a lecture. Code then picks which videos to read from your grades, across
all parts, so grade each card on its own merits and do not try to balance or ration your grades.

Each card is one YouTube video: "[id] title | channel (tier) | length | year | views", then its chapters with start
times (written by the uploader, or generated by YouTube as "key moments"; long lists show the chapters nearest this
part) and the start of its description. Tier 1 = creators of the tool or standard, universities, official channels;
2 = established educators; 3-4 = unknown.

Grades:
- 3: it clearly teaches THIS part (a chapter or the whole video is about it), from a credible source, at the level of
  final-year CS students. A university lecture or a talk by the creators whose chapter covers the part is a 3.
- 2: it very likely teaches the part in a section students could watch on its own, but the evidence is thinner (no
  chapters, a broad title) or the source is unknown.
- 1: it only touches the part, or the part is buried in a long video with no sign of where.
- 0: off-topic for this part, promotional, a reaction or news video, "for kids" or hype, or outdated when the topic is
  fast-moving (fast_moving = {{ fast_moving }}).

A video that teaches a DIFFERENT part of this lecture (see other_parts) is graded for THIS part only. Judge from the
metadata and what you know about the source; do not guess beyond them. `where`: the chapter title or start time where
the part is taught, when a chapter shows it. `standalone`: false for a live-coding stream or a podcast where the part
cannot be watched without the rest. Level note: {{ level_note }}

Grade every card, each id exactly once. Shape: {"grades": [{"video_id": "...", "grade": 2, "where": "12:40 MCP
architecture", "level_ok": true, "standalone": true, "reason": "≤ 15 words"}]}
